# Acting and Performance

## Translate intent into visible behavior

Convert an objective or impulse into actions the camera can record. Replace internal labels such as `she feels suspicious` with selected evidence such as a paused reach, a guarded shift of weight, a glance toward the exit, or a tightened grip. Use only the cues needed to make the requested change legible within the shot.

## Select cues by duration

- For a brief shot, choose one readable action and at most one supporting reaction.
- For a medium shot, connect impulse, action, and consequence through a small change in gaze, breath, posture, hands, or distance.
- For a longer shot, let the current state persist before it changes; carry posture, breath, attention, and interpersonal distance forward rather than resetting the performance.

Treat reaction as an observable response to an event. Direct gaze toward a named person, object, or landmark only when that attention matters. Use breath to support exertion, hesitation, relief, or speech without making it a mandatory beat. Use hands and posture where they are visible in the composition. Express relationship changes through approach, retreat, orientation, touch, or maintained distance.

## Character scope

Direct only characters who are present in the shot. Mention only features visible at the chosen framing and moment. Do not add off-screen reactions, hidden facial details, or body cues the camera cannot see. Preserve state inertia: a soaked coat stays heavy, a guarded stance does not vanish between shots, and a recent impact continues to affect balance until a visible recovery occurs.

## Dialogue

Keep supplied dialogue exact, including Korean wording and punctuation. Delivery may change through pace, volume, breath, hesitation, gaze, or gesture; wording may not. Use `says in Korean: "..."` when language identification is needed. If the line cannot fit the duration naturally, ask for an approved shortening, more time, or a shot split.

## Exclusions

- Do not use demographic, cultural, gendered, disability, or occupational stereotypes as acting shorthand.
- Do not require eye contact, blinking, tears, smiles, or a gaze shift in every performance.
- Do not impose a fixed number of beats; select behavior according to duration and dramatic need.
- Do not guarantee emotional realism, lip sync, facial consistency, or exact performance reproduction.
- Do not invent absent characters or invisible features to explain motivation.
